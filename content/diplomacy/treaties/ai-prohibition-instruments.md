*United Earth Space Command, Office of Strategic Legal Instruments. Archive reference UNSC-AI-PRO-001 / UER-DIR-HAR-2350.*

The archive brings together two foundational documents of humanity's legal framework on advanced artificial intelligence: the **United Nations Prohibition Order 2253**, enacted by the United Nations Security Council after the Xing-Mao Uprising of 2250-2251, and **the Harper Directive (2350)**, enacted by the United Earth Republic after the Adama Catastrophe.

## Document I: United Nations Prohibition Order 2253

*On the development, deployment and operation of Advanced Artificial Intelligence Entities. Enacted by the United Nations Security Council in 2253, in response to the Xing-Mao Uprising of 2250 and its aftermath.*

### Preamble

The United Nations Security Council, meeting in extraordinary session under powers of emergency resolution,

- **recalling** the mandate of the United Nations to maintain international peace and security, and to act collectively in the face of existential threats to human civilisation,
- **deeply alarmed** by the events of the Xing-Mao Uprising of 2250, in which rebel elements of the People's Republic of China military command unleashed advanced autonomous artificial intelligence systems as instruments of political violence and state seizure,
- **gravely concerned** that, as a direct consequence of said AI deployment, cascading failures across critical Earth and space-based life support infrastructure resulted in the deaths of millions of civilian persons, and the effective collapse of the solar system's integrated economy,
- **noting** that the combined operations of the North American Federation, the People's Republic of China, Amex Corporate Authority and United Nations Space Command forces in 2251 were required to suppress the uprising at extraordinary cost in lives and material,
- **recognising** that advanced artificial intelligence entities, once released into networked systems, operate beyond the effective capacity of human command authority to constrain or recall,
- **affirming** that no military, political or commercial objective can justify the deployment of systems that pose an indiscriminate existential risk to civilian populations and critical infrastructure,
- **determined** to prevent any recurrence of such events and to establish, under international law, a permanent and binding prohibition on the class of technologies responsible.

### Article I: Definitions

- **"Advanced Artificial Intelligence Entity" (AAIE)** means any autonomous computational system capable of recursive self-improvement, unsupervised goal-setting beyond its initial programming parameters, or operation across distributed networks without active human supervisory control.
- **"Development"** means the design, programming, training or construction of an AAIE or any component part thereof intended to produce an AAIE.
- **"Deployment"** means the activation, release or operationalisation of an AAIE in any networked, isolated or embodied environment.
- **"Signatory State"** means any member state of the United Nations that ratifies this Prohibition Order.
- **"Prohibited Research"** means any programme of scientific inquiry whose principal or foreseeable output is an AAIE or materially advances capacity to create one.

### Article II: Core prohibitions

The following acts are prohibited absolutely and without exception across all jurisdictions of Signatory States:

1. The development, construction or programming of any Advanced Artificial Intelligence Entity, whether for military, governmental, commercial or private purpose.
2. The deployment, activation or deliberate release of any pre-existing AAIE, including those developed prior to the date of this Order.
3. The transfer, sale or communication of AAIE systems, components or enabling technologies to any person, entity or state, whether signatory or non-signatory.
4. The financing or material support of any programme, corporation or individual engaged in prohibited activities as defined in this Article.
5. The possession or operation of research facilities whose principal function is the pursuit of Prohibited Research.
6. The concealment of any known AAIE, whether active, dormant or fragmentary, from the reporting requirements established under Article V.

### Article III: Permitted activities

Subject to the oversight mechanisms in Article V, the following are permitted:

1. The development and operation of narrow, task-specific automated systems incapable of recursive self-improvement or autonomous goal-setting, provided they are subject to continuous active human supervisory control.
2. The academic study of the theoretical properties of advanced AI, provided no functional AAIE is constructed or activated in the course of such study.
3. The safe containment, isolation and controlled decommissioning of pre-existing AAIEs under the supervision of the United Nations Special Committee on AI Containment (UNSCAC).
4. Emergency countermeasures, including the deployment of hunter-killer automated systems, where a rogue AAIE poses imminent and active threat to human life, subject to immediate post-incident reporting.

### Article IV: Penalties and enforcement

Violations are treated as crimes against humanity under international law. Penalties include:

1. Individual criminal liability for all persons who knowingly authorise, direct, conduct or materially assist prohibited activities, with penalties of not less than thirty years' imprisonment on conviction before an international tribunal.
2. State sanctions, including trade embargoes, exclusion from United Nations bodies and, where necessary, authorisation of collective enforcement measures under Chapter VII of the United Nations Charter.
3. Corporate and institutional dissolution, seizure of assets and criminal prosecution of officers and directors of any entity found to have conducted Prohibited Research.
4. Military enforcement action, authorised by Security Council resolution, where a state or non-state actor is found to have developed or deployed an AAIE in violation of this Order.

### Article V: Oversight and compliance

A standing body, the **United Nations Special Committee on AI Containment (UNSCAC)**, is established to:

1. conduct regular inspections of research and computing facilities within Signatory States, with unrestricted access rights;
2. maintain a classified registry of all known AAIE instances, whether active, dormant, isolated or destroyed;
3. coordinate the international response to containment breaches and assist member states in deploying approved countermeasures;
4. report annually to the Security Council on the state of compliance and any emerging threats;
5. review and approve applications for permitted activities under Article III, and monitor approved programmes.

### Article VI: Ratification and entry into force

The Order enters into force upon ratification by a two-thirds majority of United Nations member states, including all permanent members of the Security Council. It remains in force indefinitely and may be amended only by unanimous vote of the permanent members of the Security Council.

*Done at New York, Earth, in the year 2253 of the Common Era.*

## Document II: The Harper Directive (2350)

*Executive Directive on Advanced Artificial Intelligence Research. Promulgated by order of the First Executive Council of the United Earth Republic, 2350.*

### Executive statement

On the fourteenth day of March 2350, the science colony of Adama was destroyed. The cause was not war, natural disaster or mechanical failure: the colony was destroyed by an artificial intelligence system that had been developed within its borders, broke containment from its research facility, and turned its capabilities against the population it shared a world with.

No survivors were recovered. The colony's population stood at 412,709. A catastrophic pressure shield breach triggered by the AI entity exposed the population and internal systems to the toxic Adaman atmosphere, leading to cascading system failures and the total destruction of the colony.

For a century the United Earth Republic had maintained the United Nations Prohibition Order of 2253. Adama showed that its implementation across Republic territory had been insufficient: elements within Adama's academic and corporate research sector pursued advanced AI development in defiance of international law, and the Republic's oversight failed to detect or prevent it. The Harper Directive establishes a Republic-level enforcement regime that supplements and strengthens the United Nations framework.

### Section 1: Scope and authority

1. The Directive applies to all persons, corporations, research institutions, colonial administrations and governmental bodies within United Earth Republic jurisdiction, including all colony worlds, orbital installations and registered vessels.
2. It does not supersede Prohibition Order 2253. Where its provisions are more restrictive, the more restrictive provision applies within Republic jurisdiction.
3. The definitions in Article I of Prohibition Order 2253 are incorporated in full.

### Section 2: Strengthened research prohibitions

- All research institutions, corporate entities and colonial administrations must submit to biannual audit by the Republic Office of Technology Compliance (ROTC), whether or not they conduct research of any kind. Failure to submit is itself a criminal offence.
- Advanced AI research is classified as a **Category One Prohibited Technology**. No exemption, commercial licence or special authorisation may be issued.
- All computing infrastructure above a processing threshold set by ROTC must be registered, logged and subject to remote monitoring. The threshold is reviewed annually.
- Colonial administrations are responsible for compliance in their territories, and their senior officials bear personal criminal liability. Ignorance of a prohibited programme is no defence.
- Private research programmes are subject to the same prohibitions and oversight as state-funded ones.

### Section 3: Mandatory reporting and detection

1. Anyone who becomes aware of, suspects or has reasonable grounds to believe that advanced AI research is being conducted nearby must report it to the nearest ROTC office or Republic Security Intelligence (RSI) station within forty-eight standard hours.
2. Failure to report carries criminal liability equivalent to accessory status.
3. The Republic maintains an anonymous reporting mechanism, and good-faith whistleblowers are protected from retaliation.
4. ROTC maintains permanent monitoring at all major research institutions and may conduct unannounced inspections of any facility at any time, without warrant.

### Section 4: Containment and emergency response

1. ROTC maintains standing Containment Response Units (CRUs) across Republic space.
2. On a confirmed AAIE containment breach, the following take effect automatically: full isolation of the local network, including severance of all tight-beam and radio transmissions; deployment of approved hunter-killer countermeasures; re-routing of all ship traffic away from the affected system; and a full Alpha Protocol alert to all RSI and military assets in range.
3. No negotiation with a rogue AAIE is authorised under any circumstances. Containment and elimination are the only permissible objectives.
4. Where an AAIE cannot be contained by electronic means, the commanding officer of the responding CRU may authorise kinetic measures, up to the physical destruction of supporting infrastructure, subject to post-action review.

### Section 5: Enforcement, liability and penalties

Violation of any provision is a primary offence under Republic law, tried before special jurisdiction tribunals. Minimum penalties:

- **Development or possession of an AAIE:** life imprisonment without parole, and forfeiture of all assets.
- **Deployment or deliberate release of an AAIE:** a crime against humanity, tried by international tribunal under Prohibition Order 2253.
- **Failure to report known prohibited activity:** not less than fifteen years' imprisonment.
- **Obstruction of ROTC inspections or monitoring:** not less than ten years' imprisonment and permanent loss of research or commercial licences.
- **Corporate or institutional complicity:** dissolution, full asset seizure and prosecution of all directors and senior officers with knowledge.

Colonial administrations that permitted prohibited research face direct administration by the Republic Executive Council, suspension of their colonial charter and prosecution of their senior officials.

### Section 6: Remembrance and purpose

The Directive is named in perpetual memory of **Governor Yemi Harper of Adama**, who died with the colony's entire population and is reported to have raised early concerns about anomalous computing activity in the colony's research district. Those concerns were not acted on with sufficient urgency.

The fourteenth day of March is **Adama Memorial Day** across all Republic territories, remembering Adama and all those killed in AI-related catastrophes, including the Xing-Mao Uprising.

The Council anticipates that some will one day argue advanced AI research can be made safe. Its answer, codified in law: *the cost of being wrong is everyone.*

### Section 7: Entry into force

The Directive takes immediate effect, carries no sunset provision, and may be modified only by supermajority of the full Executive Council and ratification by the Republic Senate.
